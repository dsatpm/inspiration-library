The gallery name is the hero — ultra-heavy grotesk at ~9rem, white on black, wrapping to two lines and bleeding past the right edge. Above it, a small video card and a scrolling marquee of opening hours.

The transferable rule: **an institution with a strong name can use the name itself as the hero image.** Set it large enough to crop, and the letterforms become the graphic. This only works if the type is heavy enough to hold as shape — at regular weight the same size reads as a mistake.

The deliberate scale inversion is the interesting part: the *promotional* content (exhibition video, ticker) is small and sits above; the *identity* is enormous and sits below. Most sites do the reverse. Putting identity at the bottom of the first screen means the scroll starts from a position of confidence rather than a sales pitch.
