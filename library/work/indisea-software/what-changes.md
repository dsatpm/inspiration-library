Split section: argument left, an **architecture diagram** right showing `YOUR PRODUCT` → a hub marked with the company's own logo → four branches to recognisable third-party marks (ERP, accounting, eCommerce, payments).

The transferable rule: **for infrastructure products that sit between systems, a wiring diagram explains positioning faster than any amount of prose.** The company's mark is literally in the middle of the path. The value proposition is the diagram's topology — nothing needs to say "we are the integration layer."

Execution details that make it work:
- Each branch is a **different colour**, matched to the ring around its destination icon. Colour distinguishes the paths where four identical grey lines would tangle visually.
- Orthogonal routing with rounded corners — no diagonals, no crossings. Diagrams read as engineering when the lines are square and as marketing when they curve.
- Third-party logos are the **actual recognisable marks**, monochrome. Borrowing recognition from known platforms is the point; generic icons would waste it.
- The whole diagram sits on a near-white rounded card, lifting it from the warm grey page ground.

Three pill chips below the copy (`Domain expertise`, `Operational maturity`, `Partnership model`) compress the qualitative claims that the diagram cannot show.
