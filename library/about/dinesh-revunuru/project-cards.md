Two-up project cards, each on its own pale tint — sand, mint, blush, periwinkle — with a serif heading in a deeper shade of the same hue and a device mockup bleeding off the card's bottom edge.

The transferable rule: **tint each card differently but derive the heading colour from that card's own hue.** It gives a grid of unlike projects visual variety while keeping every card internally coherent. A single shared heading colour across differently-tinted cards is what makes pastel grids look cheap.

The copy pattern is worth copying verbatim: *"Customers were dropping out between the ad and the appointment. I designed and built the system that closed the gap."* **Problem, then intervention, then outcome — before any description of the artefact.** Two sentences. Most portfolio cards lead with the artefact ("a booking platform") and never state the problem; this ordering is what makes the work legible to someone who doesn't already care.

Mockups bleed past the lower card edge — same break-the-container move as [[hero]], applied at card scale.
