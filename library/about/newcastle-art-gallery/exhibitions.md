Two exhibition cards of **unequal width** sitting on a shared baseline, captions below each image rather than overlaid.

The transferable rule: **breaking a card row out of equal widths is the cheapest way to stop a grid reading as a template.** The wider card takes visual priority without needing a badge, a "featured" label, or a different treatment. Ratio here is roughly 45/55 — enough to read as intentional, not enough to look broken.

Second rule: **captions below images, not on them.** Overlaid text forces a scrim, the scrim dulls the artwork, and the artwork is the product. Below-image captions at ~1rem with a small grey metadata line keep the images at full contrast. For any gallery, portfolio, or product grid where the image is the value, this is the correct default.

Section heading is the same ultra-heavy grotesk as the wordmark, at a fraction of the size — one display face reused at different scales is the whole type system.
