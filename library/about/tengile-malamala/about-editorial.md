Asymmetric editorial grid with extreme whitespace. A small uppercase label sits alone in the top-left. The primary statement — serif, ~2.5rem — occupies the right two-thirds. A secondary paragraph at ~1rem sits far below-left, vertically offset from anything it aligns with. The image enters bottom-right.

The transferable rule: **vertical offset between columns is what makes an editorial layout feel designed rather than gridded.** If the left column's text started at the same baseline as the right column's, this becomes an ordinary two-column block. Staggering them by several hundred pixels forces a reading order (label → statement → detail → image) that a symmetric grid cannot express.

The scale relationship carries the meaning: the poetic brand statement is large serif, the operational detail is small sans. Same page, two registers, no decoration needed to separate them.
