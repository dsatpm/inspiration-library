Testimonials as full-width stacked rows rather than a card grid. Quote set large — ~2rem — on the left two-thirds; attribution pinned right at ~0.7rem uppercase.

The transferable rule: **full-width rows suit few, long, substantive quotes; grids suit many short ones.** With three quotes, a three-up grid would leave each card cramped and the row thin. Rows let each quote be set at near-headline size, which signals that these are arguments rather than decoration.

The attribution is **anonymised by role and company type** — `PRODUCT MANAGER / PE-BACKED GLOBAL TAX PLATFORM`. In enterprise B2B, clients frequently cannot be named; describing the *category* preserves credibility where a blank or a generic "a client" would destroy it. This is the correct pattern when named references are contractually unavailable.

Right-aligning attribution against left-aligned quote text creates a wide gap that reads as intentional at this scale — it works because the quote is large enough to fill its column. At body size the same layout would look broken.

Continues the site's numbered-section convention (`10 / PROOF`) — naming the section "PROOF" rather than "Testimonials" states its job.
