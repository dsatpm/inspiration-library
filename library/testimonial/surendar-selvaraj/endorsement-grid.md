Six endorsements in a 3×2 grid of subtly-lifted cards on near-black. Each card: quote at ~1rem, then an **initials disc** (`PJ`, `TN`) beside the name and role.

The transferable rule: **initials discs beat photographs in a testimonial grid.** Six mismatched headshots — different crops, lighting, and backgrounds — destroy the grid's coherence; six identical discs with consistent type keep it uniform, and the credibility comes from the name and role, not the face. It also sidesteps sourcing images you may not have rights to.

Attribution hierarchy is two steps: name in near-white, role and company in grey (`Director Of UX, Google`). **The company is doing the persuading — but it must stay subordinate to the person**, or the section reads as a logo wall pretending to be quotes.

Quotes are trimmed to roughly 15–20 words each and all land within one line of the same length, which is what lets the six cards sit on a uniform height without awkward gaps. Editing quotes to a consistent length is a layout decision, not just a copy one.

`More testimonials on LinkedIn →` below the grid caps the section at six while signalling depth — the right way to avoid an endless list.
